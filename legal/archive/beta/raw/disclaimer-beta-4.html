Hop is a travel reference, not advice. It shows general published information about visas, entry rules, travel advisories and currency, plus travel guides, to help you plan. It is not legal, immigration, financial or travel advice, and using Hop creates no advisory relationship.
Always check before you travel. Rules change, sometimes without notice, and despite careful checking any entry in Hop can be incomplete or out of date. Before you book, and again before you travel, confirm the current requirements with the destination’s embassy, consulate or official immigration website, and with your airline or other carrier, who check travel documents before you board. Every destination card links your own government’s travel advice, and Premium adds a direct link to the destination’s official visa or e-visa portal. “Last date verified” shows when Hop last checked that entry against an official source, not that the rule is still the same today.
Travel advisories. Advisory levels belong to the governments that publish them: Australia, Canada, Germany, the Netherlands, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and the United States. Hop shows each government’s advice on a simple four-step scale. Governments describe their advice in different ways: some use four levels or colours, others use warnings for all or part of a country. Where a government’s own system doesn’t match the scale step for step, the step Hop shows is Hop’s reading of that advice, not the government’s own rating, and the card says so. Always tap through and read the government’s advice itself, including any warnings for parts of a country. For Irish and South Korean passports, Hop links to the government’s travel advice instead of showing a level. India’s government publishes no comparable advisory rating, so Indian passport cards show “No advisory rating”; India’s Ministry of External Affairs may still issue advisories, so check its website too.
Exchange rates. Rates are indicative mid-market reference rates. When you’re online, Hop fetches them from ExchangeRate-API about once a day. If that isn’t available, Hop uses the European Central Bank’s reference rates for the currencies they cover and its built-in table for the rest. Offline, Hop uses the last rates it saved or its built-in table, and shows the date of the rates. They are not what a bank, card or money changer will charge you.
